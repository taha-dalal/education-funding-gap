Key milestones in education spending
Major events that shaped funding levels over three decades
1993
$5,160 per pupil. The Census Bureau begins its annual school finance survey. Wide disparities between states already exist — New Jersey spends nearly 3x what Utah spends per student.
2001
No Child Left Behind Act signed. Increased federal accountability requirements push states to boost education budgets throughout the mid-2000s. National spending accelerates.
2008
$10,297 per pupil — then the financial crisis hits. The Great Recession causes widespread state budget cuts. Many states slash per-pupil spending for 3–5 consecutive years.
2009
American Recovery & Reinvestment Act. $100 billion in federal education stimulus helps states partially offset cuts, but nominal spending still stagnates through 2013.
2013
$10,700 — barely above 2008 levels. Five years after the recession, national spending had only just recovered in nominal terms. Many states were still below their pre-recession figures.
2015
Every Student Succeeds Act replaces No Child Left Behind, giving states more flexibility. Spending begins a steady climb through the late 2010s.
2020
$13,501 — then COVID-19. Federal emergency ESSER relief funds begin flowing to schools, driving a significant acceleration in spending over the next two years.
2022
Largest year-over-year jump in 20 years: +8.9% to $15,633. Driven largely by federal ESSER COVID relief funds reaching schools. The gap between highest and lowest spending states widens further.
2024
$17,619 — highest nominal figure ever recorded. Up 6.6% from $16,526 in 2023. Despite record spending, the gap between states like New York ($31,918) and Idaho ($11,060) remains at nearly 3-to-1.

Sources: U.S. Census Bureau Annual Survey of School System Finances. Key verified figures: FY2021 $14,358 (Census, Apr 2024); FY2022 $15,633 (Census, Apr 2024); FY2023 $16,526 (Census, May 2026); FY2024 $17,619 (Census, May 2026). Pre-2020 national averages from NCES Digest of Education Statistics Table 236.60. State-level historical data from NCES Digest Table 236.75 and Census Bureau state tables. All figures nominal, not adjusted for inflation.
